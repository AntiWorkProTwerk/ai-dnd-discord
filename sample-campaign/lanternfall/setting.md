# The Fenreach

The Fenreach is a wide, slow valley of reed beds, peat cuttings, and drowned villages, walled in by low chalk hills. Forty-one years ago, on the night now called the **Lanternfall**, a shower of burning stones fell from a clear sky across the valley. Where they landed, the ground still glows faintly after rain, and the cold blue residue they left, called **glimmer**, can be scraped up, stored in glass, and burned in lanterns to work small wonders.

The valley floods a little more every year. Old farms sit on islands of raised earth, linked by timber causeways and punt channels. People here measure wealth in dry ground, good boats, and glimmer.

## Wickmere

Wickmere is the largest town in the Fenreach, about nine hundred people living on a cluster of mounds joined by bridges. Its houses stand on stilts of black oak, and its streets are part boardwalk, part canal. A sea wall of packed clay, the **Ringdike**, keeps the worst floods out, and the town depends on it completely.

Notable places:

- **The Tallow and Tide:** a warm, low-ceilinged inn on Market Mound, run by the widow **Marrow Gale**. Rooms 5 sp a night, a hot meal 2 sp. Fen travelers trade news here.
- **The Bell Stair:** an empty platform at the town's highest point where the flood-bell once hung. The bell was moved to Cray's Chapel two generations ago, when the chapel stood on higher ground than the town.
- **The Lamp House:** the hall of the Lamp Circle, a round tower with a glass roof. Glimmer vials and wardlanterns are sold here, at the prices in the rules, and nowhere else in the valley.
- **The Reeve's Hall:** a squat stone building beside the toll chain, which stretches across the main channel each night to stop boats entering unseen.
- **Pike Market:** a floating market of lashed punts selling eel, peat, reeds, rope, and rumors.

## Nearby Sites

- **Cray's Chapel:** a half-sunk chapel of the Tide Saints, three hours east by causeway (or two by punt). Its bell tower still rises above the water, and the great flood-bell inside is meant to ring whenever the Ringdike is threatened, its sound carrying to every farm in the valley. The nave is flooded waist-deep. A ferry post stands on the last dry stretch of causeway before it.
- **The Starfall Pits:** a cluster of shallow craters north of Wickmere, where the largest stones landed. Glimmer collectors work them in daylight. At night, mire-hounds den in the reeds around the pits.
- **Saltmouth Sluice:** the great wooden gate at the bottom of the valley, a day's travel south, where the Fenreach drains toward the sea. It is badly in need of repair.

## Factions

**The Lamp Circle.** Scholars and lamplighters who license glimmer collection and teach lantern casting. They are proud, careful, and short of money. Led by the elderly Keeper **Odile Fenn**. They want the bell rung and the flooding studied, and they quietly suspect that unlicensed glimmer is leaving the valley.

**The Ferrymen's Guild.** Punt-owners and channel pilots, headed by the gruff **Brannoc Vell**. They carry nearly everything that moves in the Fenreach and resent the Reeve's tolls. Rising water means more business for them, which makes townsfolk suspicious of them.

**The Reeve's Watch.** Twelve constables in oilcloth coats serving **Reeve Hask Morrow**, the elected magistrate of Wickmere. They keep the toll chain, patrol the Ringdike, and settle disputes. They are underpaid, and most of them are honest.

**The Hollow Oar (rumored).** A smuggling ring said to move stolen glimmer out of the valley by night. Most people think it is a story told to frighten apprentices.

## Money

The Fenreach uses minted coins from the hill towns:

- **Mark (gp):** a thick bronze-gold coin stamped with a lantern. Recorded as `gp`.
- **Bit (sp):** a small silver coin. Ten bits make one mark. Recorded as `sp`.

Barter is common in the outlying farms: a day's labor is worth about 3 sp, a good eel-hide 1 gp.

## Calendar and Time

The Fenreach year has twelve months of thirty days: **Thawmonth, Sowing, Greening, Reedmonth, Highsun, Haying, Harvest, Rainmonth, Mistmonth, Frostfall, Deepnight, and Candlemonth**. The adventure begins on **14 Rainmonth**, in the wettest weeks of the year, when storms come almost every evening.

Locals divide the day into **dawn, morning, midday, afternoon, dusk, and night**. Rainmonth daylight lasts about eleven hours. At night the causeways are slippery and dark, travel checks are harder, and the Watch draws the toll chain across the channel at dusk and lowers it at dawn.

## Common Knowledge

Any local character knows the following:

- The flood-bell at Cray's Chapel has not rung for three nights, even though the water has reached the bell-ropes' warning mark.
- The Lamp Circle and the Ferrymen's Guild have argued for years over who may carry glimmer.
- Mire-hounds are lean, hairless marsh beasts that hunt in packs and are afraid of bright light.
- Glimmer burns blue, smells faintly of rain on hot stone, and makes your teeth ache if you hold it too long.
