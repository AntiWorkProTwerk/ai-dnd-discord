# The Silent Bell

A short adventure for 1-4 first-level characters in the Fenreach, playable in one or two sessions. The flood-bell at Cray's Chapel has fallen silent while the water rises, and someone in Wickmere wants it to stay that way.

# Starting Situation

It is dusk on **14 Rainmonth**. Rain hammers the shutters of **the Tallow and Tide** inn in Wickmere. The characters are inside, drying off, for reasons of their own. The common room is crowded with farmers who have come in from the flooded east, and the talk is all about the bell: three nights of rising water, and Cray's Chapel has not rung once.

**Keeper Odile Fenn** of the Lamp Circle sits by the fire with a map spread across her knees. She has already asked the Reeve for help and been told the Watch "cannot spare a single constable." When the characters arrive, or as soon as one of them shows a weapon or a lantern, she invites them to her table.

**Odile's offer:** go to Cray's Chapel, find out why the bell is silent, and ring it. She pays **10 gp each now and 30 gp each when the bell rings**, plus one glimmer vial each. She can tell them the way (three hours east along the Cray causeway, or two hours by punt) and that the chapel nave is flooded.

# Scene 1: The Tallow and Tide

A social opening. Let each character introduce themselves. Useful things the characters can learn here:

- **Marrow Gale** (innkeeper) saw two Watch constables drinking with "a sharp fellow in a green oilskin" three nights ago. They left together after the toll chain was raised. (Free if asked about strangers; DC 12 persuasion if the party is rude.)
- Farmers say flat, unlit boats have been seen on the drowned east fields at night. Most assume ghosts or ferrymen.
- **Reeve Hask Morrow** arrives near the end of the scene, dripping wet, and asks loudly whether "anyone is spreading panic about bells." He tells the room the bell is "probably rusted" and that the Watch will look "when the weather clears." A DC 13 insight check notices he is nervous and keeps glancing at Odile's map.

If the party asks Hask for help, he offers them a writ to pass the toll chain (genuinely useful) and suggests they "take the causeway, not the channels." This advice steers them past the mire-hound dens.

# Scene 2: Pike Market and the Ferryman

The party will probably want a punt. **Brannoc Vell**, head of the Ferrymen's Guild, holds court on a large punt at Pike Market, even at night, under a tarp hung with lanterns. Hire costs 3 sp a day, but Brannoc refuses anyone working for "Lamp Circle snobs who call my people thieves."

**Winning Brannoc over** (any one works):
- A **DC 13 persuasion check** making clear the party is not accusing the Guild.
- Showing him that the Watch is involved (Marrow's story, Hask's nervousness): he grins, then turns serious.
- Paying 5 gp up front and promising to clear the Guild's name.
- **Intimidation (DC 15)** works, but he becomes hostile afterward and charges double.

Once won over, Brannoc shares what his pilots have seen: **a long, unlit flatboat poling east over the drowned fields each night, with a constable's lantern-signal waved from the Ringdike to let it pass.** He lends the party a punt and his nephew **Tam** (a non-combatant pilot) for free. By punt, the trip to the chapel takes two hours and avoids the causeway.

## Optional: The Causeway

If the party walks the causeway at night, the guide makes a **DC 15 survival check**. On a failure, **three mire-hounds** attack from the reeds near the Starfall Pits. Use this if the party wants a fight before the chapel or if the pace needs one.

# Scene 3: Cray's Chapel (Exploration and Puzzle)

The chapel stands in black water to its windowsills. The bell tower rises another 15 meters, and a faint blue light flickers in its top windows. A small ferry post (an empty shed) stands on the last dry patch of causeway.

**The nave** is flooded waist-deep (half movement; characters who wade in without oilcloth are `soaked`). Pews float loose. A successful **DC 12 perception check** spots fresh scrape marks on the lintel of a side window, where a boat has been tied up recently.

**The tower door** at the back of the nave is iron-banded and has no keyhole. Around it are four stone niches, each with a weathered statue of a Tide Saint that can be pressed back into the wall:

- a woman holding an open hand upward, palm wet (the **Saint of Rain**);
- a kneeling man holding a bundle of **reeds**;
- a child cupping a small **lamp**;
- an old man with a **bell** at his feet.

Carved above the door, half covered in silt: *"First the sky weeps, then the reed drinks; the lamp is lit, and last the bell speaks."*

**Solution:** press Rain, Reed, Lamp, Bell, in that order. The door grinds open.

- Clearing the silt to read the full inscription takes 10 minutes, or a DC 10 investigation check.
- A *Reveal* spell, or a DC 15 investigation check, shows that the statues have been pressed recently: the lamp-child and the bell-man are cleanest.
- **Wrong order:** a hidden channel in the floor opens and floodwater surges through the nave. Everyone in the nave makes a **DC 12 DEX check** or is knocked `prone` and `soaked`. The statues reset.
- **Alternative:** climb the outside of the tower (three DC 15 STR checks in a row, falling 1d6 damage per failure) to reach a top window. This alerts the smugglers.

# Scene 4: The Bell Loft (Combat)

A spiral stair leads up to the bell loft, a timber floor beneath the huge bronze flood-bell. **Pell Stroud** and **two Hollow Oar thugs** are here, sorting a crate of stolen glimmer vials by the light of a shuttered lantern. The bell's clapper is **wrapped in a thick wad of oiled wool and tied with wire**.

If the party opened the door quietly, they can try a **group stealth check (DC 12)** to surprise the smugglers (the surprised side loses its first turn). Otherwise roll initiative.

**Tactics:** the thugs block the top of the stair. Pell throws a lantern-oil flask on the floor (DEX DC 12 or 1d6 fire damage to one target) and then fights with his hook-knife. When he is reduced to 8 HP or fewer, he tries to jump from the window into his flatboat. If both thugs fall, he surrenders and offers to "name a bigger fish" in exchange for his freedom.

**Ringing the bell:** cutting the wire and pulling the wool free is an action and a **DC 10 STR check**. Then it takes two people pulling the rope (or one with a DC 14 STR check) to ring it. The deep tolling rolls across the whole valley, and within an hour lanterns appear on every farm and dike.

# Rewards and Aftermath

- Odile pays the promised **30 gp each** and **one glimmer vial each**.
- The **crate of 6 glimmer vials** belongs to the Lamp Circle. If returned, Odile adds 20 gp to the party's share. If kept, it is worth 180 gp, but selling it anywhere in the valley draws questions.
- Brannoc offers free punt hire for the rest of the season if the Guild's name is cleared.
- Each character earns **150 xp** for ringing the bell, plus 50 xp for exposing the Reeve with evidence, and 25 xp for solving the door without triggering the flood.
- If the Reeve is exposed, the Watch arrests him and Wickmere needs a new Reeve, a good hook for future sessions. If not, Hask quietly makes the party's life in Wickmere difficult.

# GM Only: The Secret

**Do not reveal this to players unless they discover it in play.**

Reeve **Hask Morrow** is in the pay of the Hollow Oar. Pell Stroud pays him 20 gp a week. With the east fields flooded, the smugglers' flatboats can float over the drowned causeway and go around the toll chain entirely, carrying stolen glimmer out of the valley to buyers in the hill towns. A ringing bell would bring every farmer and constable to the dikes at night, and someone would see the boats. So Hask arranged for Pell to muffle the bell.

Evidence the party can find: Pell's purse holds a folded receipt in Hask's handwriting ("For the quiet, as agreed. H."), and Pell will name Hask if he is offered his freedom. Two constables, **Dunne** and **Arlo**, wave the boats through at night. Hask also has a hidden ledger at the Reeve's Hall, if the party investigates further.

Odile does not know any of this. She suspects the Ferrymen's Guild.

# Non-Player Characters

**Odile Fenn**, Keeper of the Lamp Circle. Elderly, precise, and impatient; wipes her spectacles when worried. *Lamplighter level 4.* AC 11, HP 18, abilities str 8, dex 10, con 10, int 15, wis 16, cha 12. Lantern 6 charges; spells: Mend Flesh, Searing Mote, Reveal, Lantern Ward. Staff +1, 1d6-1. She will not travel to the chapel ("my knees and the bog disagree").

**Marrow Gale**, innkeeper of the Tallow and Tide. Broad, friendly, and nosy. AC 10, HP 9. Cudgel +2, 1d6. Knows every face in town.

**Brannoc Vell**, head of the Ferrymen's Guild. Huge, bearded, proud, quick to take offense, slow to forgive. AC 12, HP 22, abilities str 16, dex 12, con 14, int 10, wis 13, cha 11. Boathook +5, 1d8+3.

**Reeve Hask Morrow**, magistrate of Wickmere. Thin, tidy, charming in public, sweats under pressure. AC 14 (chain shirt), HP 20, abilities str 12, dex 12, con 12, int 13, wis 10, cha 14. Longblade +3, 1d8+1. Avoids fighting; calls the Watch instead.

**Pell Stroud**, captain of the Hollow Oar. Wiry, grinning, wears a green oilskin coat. AC 13, HP 24, abilities str 12, dex 16, con 13, int 12, wis 10, cha 14. Hook-knife +5, 1d6+3. *Dirty Trick* (once per fight): instead of attacking, blinds one adjacent target until the end of its next turn (DC 12 DEX check avoids it). Carries 14 gp, the receipt, and a key to his flatboat's strongbox (8 gp).

**Hollow Oar thug** (two). AC 12, HP 11, abilities str 14, dex 12, con 12. Club +4, 1d6+2. They flee if Pell flees.

**Mire-hound** (optional, three). AC 12, HP 9, abilities str 12, dex 14, con 12. Bite +4, 1d6+2. *Pack hunters:* advantage on attacks if another mire-hound is next to the target. *Light-shy:* a Lightburst or Binding Glare makes every hound that sees it roll DC 10 WIS or flee.
